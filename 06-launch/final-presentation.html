    Slide 5 · Strategy

    A journey that loses money twice.

    Chosen path: Riverty Case Study: BU Collection — A Fair Way Forward

    Problem hook

    Today's journey loses money and agent capacity at two points: before an agreement is reached, and after it. Left unchanged, costs rise with volume, portfolio value erodes, and the headline metric (SSR) can mask the problem.

    Value proposition

    Create a transparent, guided repayment journey that helps consumers choose sustainable instalment plans the first time, reducing unnecessary agent interactions while improving long-term recovery performance beyond SSR.

    Strategic crisis · 12 months of doing nothing

    Bad Consumer Feedback may lead to the loss of clients and weakens our market position. Inside of our company, there will be more failed instalment plans and less payments.

    Data-backed hypothesis

    Based on qualitative research showing that consumers abandon the journey or contact an agent because they lack confidence in choosing an affordable repayment plan, and quantitative evidence showing that 26.8% of journey drop-offs occur at the affordability step, 31% of journey starters contact an agent within 7 days, and 25% of consumers accepting the default instalment miss a payment within 60 days, I believe that helping consumers confidently choose a sustainable repayment plan for consumers with limited time and uncertain income will result in more consumers completing a sustainable self-service repayment journey without agent support, as measured by a 15% increase in the percentage of consumers who complete a self-service instalment plan without contacting an agent within 20 days. I will protect the percentage of repayment plans that miss a payment within 60 days, ensuring it does not exceed the current baseline of 19%, and will make a go/no-go decision after 8–12 weeks or once at least 1,000 consumers have completed the digital instalment journey.

    Slide 6 · Research

    The workaround is a phone call.

    Role: a consumer with limited time and an uncertain income who is trying to deal with a debt.

    Current workaround · the manual process
    External tool · Contact via telefon
    1. Open the self-service portal to check whether an instalment plan is available.
    2. Try to estimate an affordable monthly payment without enough guidance, often second-guessing or under-reporting income.
    3. Stop the journey when uncertain about the consequences or when the journey cannot accommodate their situation (e.g. unsuitable start date or missing documents).
    4. Contact a collection agent by phone to ask for reassurance or clarification before committing.
    5. Complete the repayment plan with the agent instead of through self-service.
    “They ask me what I can afford per month. My pay changes every month. Do I put my best month? My worst month? Whatever I put, I feel like I'm signing up to it forever.”Interview · P3
    Competitive advantages over the manual workaround
    • Complete a sustainable repayment plan without calling an agent for reassurance.
    • Increase long-term repayment success instead of simply maximizing self-service completion.
    • Reduce repeat contacts and manual effort while improving recovery performance beyond SSR.
    Goal

    Get a clear answer on whether a manageable payment plan is possible, and start one they can keep.

    A/B test: Payday alignment.

    Read date fixed at the end of 30 days. No results reviewed before this date.

    Hypothesis

    I believe that Payday alignment for Mara: financially constrained consumers with variable income who want to resolve their debt through self-service will result in consumers committing to an instalment plan on their own, because the first payment lands after their payday, as measured by a 9% change in the % of consumers who complete a self-service instalment plan without contacting an agent within 7 days, within 30 days. We will protect missed payments within 60 days ≤ 19% throughout the test.

    Control · A

    Consumers go through the existing flow: choose a monthly amount, provide income information and upload proof of income, then see a plan summary (amount, first payment date, frequency), review and confirm. The first payment date is set by the system as today, without asking when the consumer's income arrives and without explaining why this date was chosen.

    Not included: income-timing question, recommended first payment date, explanation of the payment date.

    Variant · B

    Same journey as Control (A), with one change: the first payment date is aligned with the consumer's payday. Before the plan summary, consumers answer “When do you usually receive your income?” (day of the month, or End of month / Middle of month / Every 4 weeks). The system recommends a first payment date 2 days after the next income date and at least 5 days from today; otherwise it uses the following income cycle, with a short, plain-language explanation of why it was chosen.

    Rule
    Ship

    if the primary metric improves by ≥ 9% at 5% significance and missed payments within 60 days remain ≤ 19% after 30 days.

    Rule
    Iterate

    if direction is positive but lift is below the MDE.

    Rule
    Kill

    if the primary metric shows no improvement or moves negatively.

    Slide 10 · Story

    What I'd tell the next PM.

    Friction point

    The hardest part was letting go of the standard GTM template. Audience, channels and tier all pushed me toward awareness and outreach, but Payday Alignment is an in-journey A/B test. A campaign would add nothing and would bias who enters the test.

    What surprised me in the review: my reach number was off (≈5,800 consumers in the variant, not 4,180), my €3.8M value case wasn't something the primary metric could prove, and “no external outreach” wasn't true because client management has to talk to creditors.

    Aha moment

    For an experiment, GTM is governance and measurement integrity.

    The riskiest “channel” isn't the consumer touchpoint but the contact centre. If agents override the recommended date or mis-tag contacts, the primary metric breaks. Launch readiness means signed thresholds, a written decision rule and stop rules, not reach.

    Key takeaways
    1. Start from the goal, not the template. If the goal is conversion inside the journey, the audience is whoever is in the journey and the GTM work is internal.
    2. Every number a stakeholder will quote needs a metric that proves it. I'd split the value case into cost-to-serve savings (measured directly) and incremental recovery (needs its own secondary metric).
    3. A bad signal only counts if it has a threshold, an owner and an action. A lagging check like 45-day contacts isn't an early warning.
    What I'd do next

    Get Legal/DPO's answer on whether a DPIA is needed (the biggest risk to the 26 Oct date), assign a builder and date to each open asset, and get the decision rule and stop rules signed at the go/no-go on 23 Oct.
